# Scan pass: hero, cards, design system, About

**Date:** 2026-08-04
**Trigger:** Leonid's review of the shipped site, reading it as a UX/UI hiring manager would on a first scan.
**Status:** executed 2026-08-04. Outcomes below differ from the plan in three
places, each noted inline. The decision record is in `_build-log.md`.

**What shipped, against what was planned:**

- **Headline.** None of the four planned candidates survived. All of them, and
  three later drafts, had the shape *proposition then claim*, which Leonid
  rejected as constructed. Shipped: `The hard part happens before anyone starts
  building.`, a point of view about the work rather than a claim about him.
- **Second hero paragraph.** Cut, as the plan's first option.
- **Card title.** V3, the bottom gradient left-aligned, with the opaque stop
  raised from 30% to 40% so the text clears the solid zone at bridge size too.
  No new scrim token was needed: the text resolves against `bg`, so the pair is
  already on the contrast table.
- **Design system.** Full restructure, as planned, plus one thing the plan did
  not anticipate: `LensChip` had to be extracted out of `ProjectTile` for the
  gallery to show the component instead of a copy of it.
- **About intro.** Candidate 2 shipped and then failed the tone check. `and I
  still use all three` claims the metalwork is live and it is not; it reads `and
  I still write the code` now.

The site is honest and well argued and it does not survive a two-second scan. Four
problems, in the order a visitor meets them.

## Decisions taken before this plan

Recorded here because they close options rather than open them, and the reasoning
matters more than the choice.

**Tier-split grid, not full width for everything.** Rollhaus and FerMentor render
full frame, GlyphsHero and How to God stay two-up below them. `Project.tier`
already carries `featured` / `bridge` with exactly that split, and
`src/content/projects.ts` records that nothing sorts by it yet. So this costs no
model change, it gives a scanner a reading order, and it keeps the page near
1900px instead of the ~3000px four full-width cards would cost.

**"One job" is cut everywhere, not reworded in one place.** Leonid's objection:
if design and implementation are one job then there is only one job, which is not
the claim. They are two ways at the same problem and he has both. The phrase
currently appears in three places (hero body, About "Where I'm going", and by
implication the h1), and fixing one leaves the site arguing with itself.

**No blame in the hero.** The current body says the hard problems were "in what
nobody had framed". Leonid's own reading is sharper: nobody held the whole
project, people started building without planning at the required capacity. True,
and unusable. A hiring manager reads any version of it as a candidate blaming his
old teams, and About already carries the braver version, where he owns the same
failure ("the temptation is to jump to a solution before the problem is properly
framed"). The meaning survives, the villain goes.

**Rejected: replacing the About intro with a "many hats" line.** The
metalworker to Unity developer to designer arc is the most memorable sentence on
the site, and "I have worn many hats" is a cliché that says less. The arc stays;
what it was missing is a claim attached to it.

**Rejected: deleting the design-system detail.** The build-time reading, the five
enforced rules and the computed contrast table are the things no other portfolio
has. The failure is ordering, not volume. Inventory moves out of the scan path
into disclosures so "nothing on this page is transcribed by hand" stays provable.

## Phase 1: Hero

`src/content/hero.ts`, `src/app/layout.tsx` (meta description),
`tests/export/static-export.test.ts`.

The headline is marked approved in the file with a do-not-reword note pointing at
`_build-log.md`. This plan is the new decision; it gets logged (Phase 5).

### What is wrong

- **h1** needs parsing at the moment a reader has two seconds, resolves into a
  truism every design system claims, reads as faintly condescending to both
  groups it names, and never says what Leonid is.
- **body 1** "from someone else's spec" reads as *he was handed tickets*, which
  spends the five years instead of banking them. "learned to do that half
  properly" also parses as *halfway properly*.
- **body 2** carries the rejected "one job" framing.

### Headline candidates

Leonid picks one. All are two-sides-of-the-same-problem rather than one merged job.

1. `Design and engineering are two ways to solve the same problem. I work in both.`
2. `Design and engineering are two angles on the same problem. I take both.`
3. `Every problem has a design answer and a build answer. I can give you both.`
4. `I solve problems from both sides, design and engineering.`

Notes: 1 and 2 name both disciplines in the first four words, which is what a
scanner needs to classify him. 3 is the most concrete and the longest. 4 is the
shortest and buries the disciplines behind an abstraction.

### Body

```
Five years of building features taught me that the expensive problems are rarely
in the code. They are in what was never decided before anyone started building,
so I went and learned to do that part.
```

Second paragraph, two options:

- **Cut it.** The h1 now carries the claim, so repeating it is redundancy. Fastest
  route from headline to work.
- **Point at evidence:** `This site is the current example. The design system it
  runs on is documented on it, generated from the same tokens the pages use.`
  Routes a scanner to the differentiator page.

### Test impact

`tests/export/static-export.test.ts` hard-codes the headline (line 25) and both
body paragraphs (lines 37, 39). All three assertions move to the new copy. Keep
them literal: the point of the guard is that copy cannot silently drift.

`src/app/layout.tsx:22` repeats the headline as the meta description. It moves too.

## Phase 2: Project cards

`src/components/project-tile.tsx`, `src/components/project-grid.tsx`,
`src/content/projects.ts`, possibly `tokens/` for a scrim.

### 2a. Summaries

Every card summary drops to one line. `Project.summary` in `types.ts` says it
should name a decision rather than describe the project, and in each case the
decision is the first clause; the rest is the payoff, which is what the case
study is for.

| Project | New summary |
|---|---|
| Rollhaus | A roller-skate configurator built on Figma variables and modes. |
| FerMentor | A fermentation app built on a model that predicts what a batch should look like right now. |
| GlyphsHero | A tactile auto-battler where the inventory is the spell. |
| How to God | VR spellcasting and grabbing for Meta Quest. I owned UX and game feel. |

Safe under `tests/unit/content.test.ts`: the guard only asserts a summary is
non-empty and is not identical to a schema line.

### 2b. Layout

`ProjectGrid` splits `projects` by `tier`:

- `featured` renders as a single-column list at full frame width (~61rem).
- `bridge` keeps the existing `auto-fill minmax(min(100%,22rem),1fr)` grid.

No extra heading between the groups. A label like "Other work" would signal
*lesser* on the two projects that carry the games and Unity half of the arc, and
the size difference already says which to read first.

`sizes` on the featured `Image` moves from `"(max-width: 48rem) 100vw, 30rem"` to
match the wider slot, or Next ships a card-sized source into a 976px box. Bridge
tiles keep the current value. Both numbers track the grid, same as the existing
comment says.

Thumb aspect stays 16:10. Rollhaus is 1120x700 and FerMentor 1121x700 natively,
so the featured pair crops to nothing.

### 2c. Title treatment — OPEN, four variants to be drafted

The title moves onto the visual and up in size from `type-subheading`.

The constraint that decides how: text over arbitrary imagery is the one contrast
this site cannot compute at build time, and a computed contrast table is its
flagship claim. The four thumbnails span near-black (GlyphsHero) to bright sand
(How to God). A translucent scrim needs roughly 85% opacity to guarantee AA
against a worst-case bright image, which erases the picture.

Variants to build and screenshot on the real Rollhaus and FerMentor thumbs at
full width:

1. **Solid band, bottom third, title centered in it.** Contrast fully computable,
   joins the measured table.
2. **Full translucent scrim, title dead centre.** What was originally asked for.
   Costs the measured claim for this pair; the design-system page would have to
   state the exception.
3. **Bottom gradient scrim, title bottom-left.** Editorial standard, computable in
   the dense zone.
4. **Solid plate across the vertical middle, title centered.** Poster framing,
   computable, covers the middle of the image.

Whichever wins, the title stays a real `<h3><a>` so the card keeps its accessible
name and its place in screen-reader heading navigation. If a scrim token is
needed it is minted properly: a Brand token, a Semantic token, an entry in
`SEMANTIC_COLOURS`, and a `CONTRAST_PAIRS` row. `tests/unit/design-system.test.ts`
guards that set in both directions, so an undocumented token fails the build,
and `token-discipline` bans the colour literal that would be the shortcut.

## Phase 3: Design system

`src/app/design-system/page.tsx`, `src/content/design-system.ts`,
`src/components/design-system/*`.

### The diagnosis

The page renders 12 primitive swatches, 16 brand swatches, 9 semantic swatches,
then 11 type roles each with five token property lines, roughly 55 lines of token
names. That is inventory. It answers *what tokens exist* when the question a UX/UI
reader has is *what decisions hold this together, and what did they buy*.

The larger gap: **there is not a single component on the page.** No button, no
chip, no focus state, no hover. A design-system page with zero components reads as
a token dump.

### New structure

1. Intro and restraint line. Unchanged.
2. **Pillars.** New. Three or four, each one sentence plus its proof: Semantic is
   the only layer a component may touch; a type role carries five decisions rather
   than one; the rules run in the test suite; contrast is computed rather than
   asserted.
3. **The system in place.** New. Every control the site actually has, in one place:
   link, lens chip, theme toggle, project card, contact link, nav item, with
   default / hover / focus states. Framed as proof of the restraint claim already
   on the page, because *these are all of them* is a stronger sentence than the
   claim on its own.
4. **Three layers, one direction.** Prose kept. The 28 swatches are replaced inline
   by one traced chain (`neutral-900` to `brand-ink` to `color-fg`), which shows
   the argument better than the grids do. Full grids move behind `<details>`.
5. **Space, type and radius.** Specimens kept, since showing the scale is the design
   work. The five token property lines per role move behind a disclosure.
6. **Rules.** Unchanged.
7. **Contrast.** Unchanged. This is the flagship.
8. **How it is built.** Unchanged.

### Open question for the executing step

Hover and focus cannot be shown statically without duplicating the styles as
forced classes, which would mean the gallery documents a copy of the system
rather than the system. Preference: render each control live and label it, since
the page is live and the reader can hover it, plus one row with focus rings forced
on. Decide at build time.

### Test impact

`tests/export/design-system.test.ts` requires every `SEMANTIC_COLOURS` utility and
every `TYPE_ROLES` utility to appear in the exported body. `<details>` still emits
its contents, so disclosures are safe. Removing a specimen outright is not.

## Phase 4: About

`src/content/about.ts`, `tests/export/about.test.ts` (follows the module, no edit
expected).

### Intro

Two candidates, both keeping the arc:

1. `I'm a UX/UI designer in Berlin, and I build what I design. Before this I spent
   five years as a Unity developer, and before that I was a metalworker.`
2. `I'm a UX/UI designer in Berlin. I got here through five years as a Unity
   developer and, before that, a metalworker's apprenticeship, and I still use all
   three.`

2 is the honest form of the many-hats claim: it says the range is still in use
rather than that it exists.

### Section order

Now: How that happened, Why UX, How I work, Where I'm going. Chronological, which
buries the present.

New: **How I work, Where I'm going, Why UX, How I got here.** Present tense first,
then the explanation a hiring manager actively wants (why is a Unity developer
applying for UX), then the story.

"How that happened" is renamed, because as the last heading its "that" has no
antecedent.

### Two line edits

**"How I work" opening.** It currently reads "I think in systems. That means I am
usually not the fastest person on a task, and I am reliably the one whose work
still holds up when the requirements change three months later." Good writing, and
as the first sentence of the page it opens on a weakness. Flip the clauses so the
strength lands first. Both halves stay.

**"Where I'm going" opening.** "Design and engineering as one job rather than two"
carries the framing this plan rejects. It becomes two ways at the same problem, to
match the new headline.
